# Insurance innovation, reporting and analysis: literature review and implications

Searched 16 Oct 2026. **Evidence grades:** **[P]** primary document, relevant sections read; **[S]** secondary (law-firm, consultancy, trade press or search-result summary; primary not opened); **[U]** could not be verified. Most [S] sources are commercial publishers with an interest in the topic, so read them as informed but not neutral. Every claim below carries a grade; where I could not open the primary text I say so.

## 1. Headline findings
1. **Regulators have converged on principles, not rules, for insurer AI.** The one primary text read in full (EIOPA, Aug 2025) asks for a risk-based, proportionate framework: assess each AI use case's impact, then scale data governance, human oversight, explainability, record keeping, fairness monitoring and redress to that impact [P]. IAIS (July 2025) and the NAIC model bulletin say the same thing in different words [S]. India has no AI rulebook yet: IRDAI set up a working group in June 2026 that is due to report within three months [S].
2. **Fairness is a genuine tension, and the regulators say so.** EIOPA's own annex warns that group-fairness metrics can contradict actuarial fairness (same risk, same price) and tells firms to choose metrics with care [P]. Academic work on discrimination-free pricing shows proxy discrimination can only be removed if the protected attribute is available when the model is fitted, and that a small portfolio-level effect can hide a large effect on a subgroup [S].
3. **Reporting has moved from "get compliant" to "use the data".** Year three of IFRS 17 is described as stable, with finance functions shifting toward analytics and automation, though disclosure comparability is still uneven [S]. Two dated changes matter for planning: Solvency II amendments apply from 30 Jan 2027 [S], and Indian insurers move to Ind AS-based reporting from 1 Apr 2026 with an optional one-year forbearance and two years of parallel reporting [S].
4. **GenAI in claims and underwriting is in production, but the evidence is anecdotal.** Analyst trackers report generative and agentic AI as a majority of new insurance AI deployments [S]; I could not verify headline consulting-firm figures such as shareholder-return multiples [U]. Independent studies on document extraction point to one recurring failure: unsupported values, especially amounts that cannot be matched to the source text, sometimes stated with high confidence [S].
5. **Climate and natural-catastrophe scenario analysis is now routine in ORSA**; the open questions are capital treatment and modelling quality [S].
6. **"Open insurance" and parametric products are slower than the hype.** The EU open-finance proposal (FiDA) is stalled in negotiation [S]; India's Bima Sugam marketplace has missed several launch dates [S]; the parametric sources I found are old (2011, 2017), so I make no claim about recent growth [S].

## 2. Themes in detail

### 2.1 AI governance and supervision
| Source | What it says | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| EIOPA Opinion on AI governance and risk management (EIOPA-BoS-25-360, 6 Aug 2025) | Interprets Solvency II, IDD and DORA for AI systems outside the AI Act's prohibited and high-risk categories. Step 1: assess impact (data scale and sensitivity, customers affected incl. vulnerable, autonomy, consumer-facing, financial-inclusion or compulsory lines, prudential effect). Step 2: proportionate measures. Expects data governance incl. bias removal and proxy checks, monitoring with fairness metrics, redress, meaningful explainability, record keeping (example record pack in annex), performance metrics for drift, resilience to poisoning and adversarial attack with fallback plans. EIOPA will review supervisory practice two years after publication. | [P] |
| IAIS Application Paper on supervision of AI (final July 2025) | Applies existing Insurance Core Principles; five areas: proportionality, governance and accountability, robustness and security, transparency and explainability, fairness, ethics and redress. Non-binding. | [S] |
| NAIC model bulletin on insurers' use of AI (Dec 2023) | Expects a written AI programme covering governance, risk management and internal audit; adopted in roughly half the US states by 2025; Colorado goes further. No visible enforcement as of mid-2025 per one firm. The bulletin PDF I fetched had no extractable text. | [S] |
| Colorado SB 21-169 and its regulations | Governance framework adopted Sept 2023; a draft life-insurance regulation proposed annual quantitative testing for race/ethnicity disparities in decline rates and premiums using inferred race (BIFSG) and regression. Final status not confirmed. | [S] |
| EU AI Act | Life and health risk assessment and pricing for natural persons is high-risk; fraud detection and prudential capital calculation are carved out; deployers owe a fundamental-rights impact assessment. **Sources conflict on the application date (Aug 2026 vs Aug 2027)** and I did not check later amendments. | [S] |
| IRDAI AI working group (17 Jun 2026) | Remit: AI inventory, governance maturity, explainability, AI-driven cyber risk, stress testing, audit; claims and fraud named as priorities. No published rulebook found. | [S] |

**Reading across:** an inventory, a risk tier per use case, human oversight scaled to impact, auditable records, fairness monitoring, and a redress route are common to all of them. That is a design brief, which is why this repo now has an inventory and tiering module (`ai/governance.py`) and fairness metrics (`ai/fairness.py`).

### 2.2 Reporting and regulatory change
- **IFRS 17**: stabilised by year three; the contractual service margin makes profit emergence more predictable; limited effect so far on strategy and capital management, which stay driven by solvency metrics; implementation lessons were scale, timeline and interpretation differences [S: PwC, SOA citing Fitch, Moody's, KPMG].
- **Solvency II review (Directive (EU) 2025/2)**: applies from 30 Jan 2027; proportionality for small and non-complex undertakings; risk-margin cost-of-capital rate falls from 6% to 4.75%; most annual reporting deadlines extended, quarterly unchanged [S: William Fry, Deloitte, Milliman].
- **Ind AS 117 (India)**: applies to non-insurers since 1 Apr 2024; insurers follow an IRDAI roadmap with an effective date of 1 Apr 2026, forbearance to 31 Mar 2027 on a board-approved plan, parallel reporting for at least two years, and 11 insurers reported as implemented by Sept 2026 [S: secondary reproductions of IRDAI circulars; confirm against the circulars].
- **Climate**: EIOPA reports most insurers now include climate scenarios in ORSA (physical and transition); IAIS published a comprehensive climate application paper in April 2025; proposed capital adjustments for transition risk await a Commission decision [S].

### 2.3 Analytics and data
- **Document AI evidence**: a legal-document study for insurance costs found a small open model hallucinated materially, reduced by fine-tuning, and that segment retrieval beat a classic baseline; an enterprise-oversight paper with a claims case traced a poor hallucination rate to claim-amount extraction where values could not be matched to source and were returned with high stated certainty; an insurance-PDF benchmark found content-aware chunking improved accuracy and attribution; a regulatory-extraction study argues models invent plausible values when information is absent [S: arXiv abstracts and summaries]. *Implication:* verbatim-evidence grounding and calibration reporting are the right controls.
- **Pricing fairness**: marginalising the protected attribute out of a best-estimate model removes direct and proxy discrimination (Lindholm, Richman, Tsanakas, Wüthrich, ASTIN Bulletin 52(1), 2022); EU law already requires gender-neutral pricing, and the authors note regulators give little guidance on proxy discrimination [S].
- **Telematics**: US states are moving to statute (consent, voluntary enrolment, disclosure of premium effects); consumer groups ask the NAIC for guidance; one survey cited found only 13% of Maryland drivers enrolled in 2023 [S].
- **Ecosystem**: FiDA's insurance scope excludes health and life in the draft and the negotiation has stalled; Bima Sugam's first products were expected around Sept 2026 and I could not confirm launch [S].
- **Hype check**: "one in three insurers has an AI agent in production" and "68% of deployments are generative or agentic" come from a vendor blog and an analyst tracker respectively [S]; treat as directional.

## 4. Limits of this review
- Search-based and dated 16 Oct 2026; not a systematic review. No access to paywalled sources.
- **Not retrieved or unverified:** the NAIC bulletin text, the full IAIS paper, IRDAI circulars, the final status of the Colorado testing rule, the AI Act application date, recent Swiss Re sigma reports, nat-cat model science, any BCG material, and the consulting-firm statistics quoted second-hand.
- The EIOPA opinion was read from a mirror copy hosted by an Italian legal portal (the PDF text matches the document number and date); confirm against EIOPA's own site before citing formally.
- Several sources are vendor or Big Four marketing; none is peer-reviewed except the actuarial paper.
